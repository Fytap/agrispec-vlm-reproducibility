\title{Bounded Instance-Support Evaluation for Finite Crop--Weed Review Queues}

\begin{abstract}
Fixed-capacity review is useful only when distinct plants remain represented after candidate formation, role qualification, ranking, and truncation. We present an evaluation contract for finite crop--weed review queues that separates these losses. At a fixed queue capacity and matching threshold tuple, one-to-one support measures the maximum number of distinct candidate--plant records, whereas set coverage measures the union of truth instances incident to qualified candidates. These are threshold-conditioned geometric endpoints, not bounds on human recovery. On the official WeedsGalore split, proposal operating-point commissioning increased raw spatial support but produced ranker- and contract-dependent queued effects. Under the commissioned-only ranker, one-to-one support increased by 0.0187 at matched burden. Mask R-CNN had the highest point estimate at $K=20$ (0.2159), although crossed seed-by-image intervals for its contrast with U-Net included zero. Standard AP, AR, and a queue-level PQ-style diagnostic did not expose the 105-instance merge-capacity gap. Two independent operators showed high category agreement, while human reviewability and mask qualification remained non-equivalent. A locked transfer to 60 CWFID images produced no candidates from five U-Net checkpoints; post-lock continuous scores showed weak, checkpoint-variable ranking and poor role qualification at low thresholds. The contract therefore supports executable offline quality assurance while making model, matching, and transfer boundaries explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Site-specific weed management links sensing, plant localisation, crop--weed discrimination, and intervention \cite{slaughter2008robotic,swinton2005economics}. Field-scale mapping protocols must align imaging, ground validation, and the management question \cite{casanova2025protocols}. The same alignment is needed when predicted regions enter an offline review queue. A high pixel score does not establish how many distinct plants remain available when only a finite number of regions can be inspected.

Standard instance metrics answer complementary questions. COCO-style average precision and average recall evaluate detection and mask quality across intersection-over-union thresholds and detection limits \cite{lin2014coco}. Panoptic Quality combines recognition and segmentation quality after one-to-one matching \cite{kirillov2019panoptic}. Recent analyses show that average precision can obscure duplicate predictions and can change abruptly with matching thresholds \cite{jena2023beyondmap,chen2023sortedap}. These metrics are essential for model comparison, but they do not encode an agricultural review budget or the sequence by which a plant disappears from a queue.

Finite review introduces several loss mechanisms. A plant can be absent from the proposal pool, contained in a mixed crop--weed region, merged with neighbours, ranked below the cut-off, or duplicated by several candidates. Object-proposal research relates recall to overlap and proposal count \cite{hosang2016proposals}. Precision-at-the-top methods address ranking under restricted capacity \cite{kar2015precision}. An operational audit still needs a contract that connects proposal support, role evidence, multiplicity, ranking, and the number of records available for inspection.

The queue studied here supports offline annotation quality assurance and model-failure review. An operator can retain a region, reject it, or escalate an ambiguous mixture. The queue does not issue an actuation command. This task differs from active learning, which selects observations for annotation or retraining \cite{sheikh2020gradient,vanmarrewijk2024active}; selective prediction, which abstains from automated outputs \cite{geifman2017selective,geifman2019selectivenet}; and learning to defer, which assigns cases between a model and an expert \cite{mozannar2020defer,mozannar2023whoshould}.

We present a bounded instance-support evaluation for finite crop--weed review queues. It has four contributions. First, it separates spatial, role-qualified, set-coverage, and one-to-one endpoints. Second, it tests proposal effects across 27 matching contracts and four ranker-training schemes. Third, it combines seed and test-image variation and compares the queue endpoint with standard AP, AR, and PQ-style diagnostics. Fourth, it records human category agreement and a locked cross-dataset transfer. The contribution is an agricultural evaluation contract and evidence decomposition, not a new segmentation architecture or an operational control system.

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Review task and endpoint bounds}

Each review item contained one RGB tile, one highlighted connected region, and a local RGB view. The intended output was one quality-assurance record: retain, reject, or escalate. Each tile contributed at most $K=20$ regions. This value standardised capacity across systems. It was not derived from operator time, labour cost, or hectares covered.

One-to-one support reflects the record contract. For a fixed queue capacity $K$ and matching threshold tuple, a selected region can contribute at most one edge to the maximum-cardinality candidate--instance matching. Set coverage is the number of truth instances incident to at least one edge under that same tuple. The first endpoint is sensitive to duplicate and merged regions; the second retains every qualified membership and is therefore merge-tolerant. Their ordering is a property of the thresholded bipartite graph. Neither endpoint estimates, lower-bounds, or upper-bounds the number of plants that a person could recover from a region; plant-count extraction was not measured.

Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} separates inference from offline scoring and places the endpoints within the evidence chronology. Target truth can enter declared training or validation roles. Test masks and instance identifiers cannot enter proposal generation, ranking features, or queue allocation. Taxonomic identity, agricultural role, and management morphotype remain distinct axes. The experiments evaluate only the crop--weed role axis.

\subsection{Datasets, splits, and evidence classes}

The source specialists used public SugarBeets2016 RGB images and masks \cite{chebrolu2017sugarbeets}. Target development used 156 annotated $600\times600$ pixel WeedsGalore tiles from one maize field \cite{celikkan2025weedsgalore}. The official allocation contains 104 training, 26 validation, and 26 test tiles. Raster enumeration yielded 2,168 crop and 10,029 weed instances. The official test masks contained 256 crop and 1,712 weed instances.

The test split represents four acquisition dates (Table~\ref{tab:dates}). The first three dates contribute eight tiles each. The last date contributes two. Date-block resampling treats the four dates as observed groups, but their physical independence is unknown. Capture indices cannot be mapped to verified field units. Image-bootstrap intervals therefore describe variation within this official image set, not a farm population.

Evidence was assigned to three analysis classes before interpretation. The frozen primary experiment transferred five validation-selected U-Net checkpoints to CWFID. The matched-burden, strong-model, ranker-source, contract-grid, merge-aware, operator, and standard-metric analyses are post-test engineering audits on the official WeedsGalore split. Threshold sweeps of CWFID scores are post-lock diagnostics. Post-test and post-lock analyses are descriptive and exploratory. They cannot restore a previously unseen confirmatory status.

Cross-dataset evaluation used the official CWFID v1.0 release \cite{haug2015cwfid}. Its 60 RGB images and public annotations were fixed at source commit \texttt{176612f3}. The protocol fixed preprocessing, five existing U-Net checkpoints, validation-selected settings, $K=20$, and endpoints before data access. All 242 upstream blobs were verified before one recorded opening. Weed truth units were eight-connected annotation components of at least 16 pixels. They are component proxies, not persistent plant identities. No CWFID label was used for fitting or selection.

\subsection{Candidate formation, rankers, and model families}

Two ResNet-18 U-Net-style source specialists were initialised with ImageNet-1K weights \cite{he2016resnet}. One estimated plant foreground and the other estimated crop and weed probabilities. Thresholding foreground probability at $T$, applying eight-connectivity, and removing components below $A$ pixels defined setting $(T,A)$. Full training details appear in the supplement.

The original operating point was $(0.95,32)$. Proposal operating-point commissioning searched 12 settings on the 104 official-training tiles. Feasible settings produced no more than 100 candidates per tile on average. This limit bounded offline feature extraction and prevented unconstrained proposal expansion. It was deliberately wider than $K=20$, which defined the downstream review capacity. Training role-qualified proposal recall was maximised, followed by lower burden, higher threshold, and larger area. The selected setting was $(0.70,32)$.

Five candidate rankers used source weed score, geometry, role-plus-local features, frozen DINOv2 features, or component-masked DINOv2 features \cite{oquab2024dinov2}. Logistic regularisation was selected on official validation. The burden-matched proposal audit tested four training schemes: commissioned-only, original-only, the union of both pools, and reciprocal cross-pool transfer. The reciprocal scheme trained on one pool and applied that ranker to the other pool.

Target semantic models comprised a ResNet-18 U-Net and DeepLabV3+ with a ResNet-50 encoder \cite{chen2018deeplabv3plus}. The instance model was Mask R-CNN with a ResNet-50 FPN backbone \cite{he2017maskrcnn}. U-Net used five training seeds. DeepLabV3+ and Mask R-CNN used three. Validation selected each checkpoint and component policy. All model families used the same $K=20$ endpoint. Training used one NVIDIA H200 accelerator per seed.

\subsection{Support, matching, and standard metrics}

For truth instance $I_j$ and candidate $C_i$, instance coverage is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{cov}(I_j,C_i)=\frac{|I_j\cap C_i|}{|I_j|}.
\end{equation}
Spatial support requires at least one candidate with coverage of 0.50. Role-qualified support additionally requires 0.50 labelled-pixel coverage and 0.90 same-role purity. These values form the primary matching contract. A $3\times3\times3$ grid used instance coverage, labelled coverage, and purity values of 0.25, 0.50, and 0.75, with 0.90 replacing 0.75 at the highest purity level.

For a selected queue $Q_K$ and primary threshold tuple $\boldsymbol{\tau}=(0.50,0.50,0.90)$, let $E_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}$ contain every candidate--weed-instance pair meeting the instance-coverage, labelled-coverage, and same-role-purity thresholds. Set coverage is $|\{I_j:\exists C_i\in Q_K,(C_i,I_j)\in E_{\boldsymbol{\tau}}\}|$. One-to-one support is the maximum-cardinality matching on the same thresholded graph. Candidate and instance identifiers resolve ties. The difference between these numerators is the merge-capacity gap after duplicate memberships are removed. Candidate precision is the fraction of selected regions satisfying the role-qualified weed contract. Every reported endpoint is indexed by $K$ and $\boldsymbol{\tau}$; changing either changes the estimand.

Crop exposure is the fraction of tiles with a queued region overlapping crop truth. We report any positive overlap and candidate-area overlap thresholds of 1\% and 10\%. Standard diagnostics include mAP over IoU 0.50--0.95, AP50, AP75, AR100, and queue AR20. A PQ-style diagnostic is computed on the predicted-weed subset of the $K=20$ queue. It is not full panoptic quality because background and crop segments do not form a complete panoptic partition.

\subsection{Matched burden and uncertainty}

Commissioning changes candidate support and burden. A common per-tile capacity used
\begin{equation}
k_i=\min(20,n_i^{\mathrm{original}},n_i^{\mathrm{commissioned}})
\end{equation}
for test tile $i$. Both settings contributed 471 candidates. Ranker-free proposal-set ceilings and per-tile $K=20$ one-to-one oracles separate formation capacity from achieved ranking.

The primary model comparison uses 20,000 crossed bootstrap replicates. Each replicate samples a training seed within each model and resamples the 26 test images with replacement. The two prespecified one-to-one contrasts are DeepLabV3+ minus U-Net and Mask R-CNN minus U-Net. We report 95\% intervals and Bonferroni-adjusted familywise 97.5\% intervals. A date-block plus seed bootstrap is a sensitivity analysis over four observed dates. No interval is interpreted as a farm-population interval.

\subsection{Two-operator technical review}

Two different human operators independently reviewed the frozen 518-region U-Net queue. They used common written instructions and 12 practice items from validation data. They did not discuss formal items, view peer responses, use automated labels, or conduct external searches. Acquisition date, tile identity, candidate identifiers, scores, ranks, model identity, and mask-derived truth were hidden.

The operators recorded primary content, weed reviewability, crop presence, localisation usability, and five-point confidence. Timing was not collected or analysed. Exact agreement, Cohen's $\kappa$, Gwet's AC1, and scene-cluster intervals were computed. Linear and quadratic weighted $\kappa$ were added for ordered fields. Human consensus and mask qualification were compared in a three-by-two contingency table over all 518 regions. Binary precision, recall, F1, Jaccard, and accuracy were computed only on regions with determinate consensus (both ``yes'' or both ``no''); disputed or uncertain regions were retained in the contingency table and excluded from those binary denominators.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Commissioning expanded raw support, while queued effects depended on ranker training}

The original setting produced 802 test candidates, or 30.85 per tile. The commissioned setting produced 2,351, or 90.42 per tile. Spatial proposal recall increased from 0.2973 to 0.5713. Role-qualified proposal recall increased from 0.1928 to 0.3026. These ranker-free changes establish greater raw geometric support.

At the common 471-candidate burden, the commissioned-only ranker increased spatial recall by 0.0818, set coverage by 0.0374, and one-to-one recall by 0.0187. Precision decreased by 0.0679. The primary-contract one-to-one change was 0.0210 with the union ranker, $-0.0514$ with the original-only ranker, and $-0.0625$ under reciprocal transfer (Table~\ref{tab:commissioning}). Commissioning therefore did not produce a ranker-independent queue improvement.

\subsection{Set coverage exposed a merge-capacity gap}

The 518-region U-Net queue contained 170 regions with no qualified weed instance, 286 with one, and 62 with two or more. One merged region covered nine qualified instances. The union of qualified memberships contained 453 distinct instances. One-to-one matching retained 348. Set coverage was therefore 0.2646, whereas one-to-one support was 0.2033. The 105-instance difference arose from merged capacity; no duplicate membership remained after the set union.

Among 301 consensus-reviewable regions, set coverage was 0.2021 and one-to-one support was 0.1454 under the primary threshold tuple. Their 97-instance graph gap again reflected merges. Reviewability increased with geometric multiplicity: 0.3059 for no qualified instance, 0.6818 for one, 0.8140 for two, and 1.0000 for three or more. Merged regions can be useful evidence, but this association does not convert set coverage into a human-recovery endpoint because the operators did not count independently recoverable plants.

\subsection{Standard metrics and crossed uncertainty changed the model comparison}

Mask R-CNN had the highest one-to-one point estimate at $K=20$: $0.2159\pm0.0038$ across three seeds. U-Net reached $0.2027\pm0.0057$ across five seeds, and DeepLabV3+ reached $0.1924\pm0.0032$ across three. The crossed seed-by-image contrast was $+0.0132$ for Mask R-CNN versus U-Net. Its 95\% interval was $-0.0009$ to 0.0300. The familywise interval was $-0.0027$ to 0.0329. DeepLabV3+ minus U-Net was $-0.0103$, with a 95\% interval of $-0.0237$ to 0.0026. Neither contrast excluded zero.

The date-block plus seed interval was 0.0023 to 0.0341 for Mask R-CNN minus U-Net and $-0.0244$ to 0.0133 for DeepLabV3+ minus U-Net. These four-date intervals were more favourable to Mask R-CNN but remain conditional on the observed dates and limited seed counts. We therefore report Mask R-CNN as the highest point estimate, not as a statistically established winner.

Standard metrics on the same predictions also favoured Mask R-CNN (Table~\ref{tab:models}). Its mAP was 0.1064 and AP50 was 0.3189. U-Net reached 0.0853 and 0.2616, respectively. At the queue limit, Mask R-CNN reached AR20@50 of 0.1861 and a PQ-style score of 0.1936. The matching-contract grid was less uniform: Mask R-CNN ranked first in 21 of 27 contracts, while U-Net ranked first in six. U-Net led when instance coverage was 0.75 with labelled coverage of 0.25 or 0.50.

\subsection{Operators agreed on categories, but human and geometric criteria differed}

Both operators completed all 518 regions. Weed-reviewability agreement was 0.9421, Cohen's $\kappa$ was 0.8930, and Gwet's AC1 was 0.9206. The scene-bootstrap interval for AC1 was 0.8918 to 0.9483. Primary content, crop presence, and localisation AC1 values were 0.9355, 0.9341, and 0.9243.

Confidence matched exactly for 0.9440 of items. Linear weighted $\kappa$ was 0.9366 and quadratic weighted $\kappa$ was 0.9589. Mean confidence was 3.523 for one operator and 3.510 for the other. Both medians were 4, with interquartile ranges of 3--4. Among 445 determinate localisation pairs, exact agreement was 0.9955 and quadratic weighted $\kappa$ was 0.9966.

The operators reached consensus that 301 regions were reviewable and 143 were not. Seventy-four were disputed or contained uncertainty. Mask qualification agreed with consensus reviewability on 249 positive and 76 negative regions. It produced 67 mask-positive/human-negative and 52 mask-negative/human-positive regions. Thirty-nine unresolved regions were mask-positive and 35 were mask-negative. On the 444 determinate-consensus regions (85.71\% of all items), mask precision was 0.7880, recall was 0.8272, F1 was 0.8071, Jaccard was 0.6766, and accuracy was 0.7320. Human reviewability and fixed geometry are related but non-equivalent evidence.

\subsection{Locked CWFID transfer failed before queue formation}

The locked primary CWFID evaluation contained 60 images and 331 weed-component proxies. Every U-Net checkpoint produced zero candidates at its frozen validation-selected threshold. Thus no queue item or component recovery was observed. The empirical all-zero bootstrap was degenerate and is not reported as a 0--0 population interval. For candidate occurrence across 60 images, the one-sided 95\% binomial reference upper bound was 0.0487. This bound is not a component-recall or farm-population guarantee.

Post-lock diagnostics retained the frozen result and did not select an external threshold. Image-macro plant-pixel AUROC ranged from 0.3871 to 0.6374 across checkpoints. Pixel AP ranged from 0.0438 to 0.0715. These scores show weak and checkpoint-variable ranking. Calibration bins placed nearly all pixels below 0.05, while observed plant prevalence in the lowest bin was about 0.055.

At diagnostic threshold 0.01, spatial recall ranged from 0.0363 to 1.0000. Role-qualified one-to-one support was nonzero for only one checkpoint and reached 0.0423. Some thresholds generated broad or mixed regions, including one checkpoint with spatial recall 1.0 but qualified recall 0. This pattern does not isolate a single failure mechanism. Threshold mismatch, role semantics, scale, viewpoint, crop type, and annotation proxies may all contribute.

\section{Discussion}

The evaluation contract reveals information that candidate discrimination and standard instance scores do not provide alone. AP, AR, and PQ-style diagnostics measure localisation, segmentation, ranking, and duplicate errors under established conventions. The finite-queue contract adds an explicit agricultural capacity and decomposes losses before and after that capacity. Set coverage and one-to-one support further separate merged capacity from duplicate capacity.

The merge-aware analysis distinguishes two graph quantities under the primary threshold tuple and $K=20$. One-to-one support is the maximum number of candidate--plant pairs that can be assigned without reusing either node. Set coverage is the truth-instance union over all qualified edges and therefore retains multiple memberships of a merged candidate. Their 105-instance difference quantifies merge capacity in this thresholded graph. It is not a range for human performance: geometric overlap does not establish visual separability, and the operator protocol did not collect plant counts.

Proposal operating-point commissioning improved ranker-free spatial and role-qualified support. Its queued benefit depended on which proposal distribution trained the ranker. Commissioned-only and union training produced positive median effects, whereas original-only and reciprocal transfer reversed them. This result distinguishes a proposal ceiling from an achieved queue. A larger candidate pool can contain more threshold-qualified support while remaining poorly ordered by a mismatched ranker.

The model comparison is similarly conditional. Mask R-CNN led most matching contracts and all reported standard metrics. Crossed uncertainty still included zero for its one-to-one contrast with U-Net. Only three Mask R-CNN seeds and five U-Net seeds were available. The four acquisition dates came from one field. These results support a model-ranking hypothesis for future evaluation, not a general population claim.

The two-operator study establishes that the review categories can be applied consistently by these two operators. It does not establish operator-population performance, plant-count recovery, review speed, or labour economics. Disputed and uncertain regions would enter the escalation branch of the workflow. On determinate consensus items, the human--mask Jaccard index of 0.6766 also shows why geometric qualification should remain an offline measurement rather than a surrogate for human usefulness.

The CWFID experiment defines a clear transfer boundary. The frozen system failed before queue construction. Continuous diagnostics found some ranking signal in selected checkpoints but weak AP and unstable threshold behaviour. The evidence cannot assign the failure solely to calibration. External evaluation covered U-Net because those five checkpoints and their thresholds were fixed before CWFID opening. Mask R-CNN and semantic reordering were developed after official-test access and were not inserted retrospectively into the locked protocol.

The contract can enter an executable annotation-QA workflow. A team first fixes a candidate source, role criterion, review budget, and escalation rule. It then measures proposal ceilings, achieved set and one-to-one support, crop exposure, and standard AP/AR. Ranker-source and contract grids identify fragile improvements. A separately locked acquisition tests whether the queue still forms. Timing, plant-count extraction, and cost can be added as independent workflow endpoints when collected.

\subsection{Limitations}

WeedsGalore supplies one field and four acquisition dates. Capture groups do not identify independent plots. All post-test WeedsGalore extensions are exploratory engineering audits. Image and date-block intervals do not support farm-level inference. CWFID differs in crop, sensor geometry, scale, and annotation contract, and its components are plant proxies. The operator study involved two fixed individuals and omitted timing and recoverable-plant counts. The $K=20$ capacity is experimental rather than economically optimised. Public weights and datasets support computational replay, but full retraining requires the upstream data and model assets listed in the archive.

\section{Conclusion}

Bounded instance-support evaluation connects crop--weed predictions to a finite offline review queue. Spatial and role-qualified proposal support locate formation losses. At fixed queue capacity and matching thresholds, set coverage and one-to-one matching expose merge capacity under alternative graph assignments. Contract and ranker grids reveal conditional improvements, while crossed uncertainty prevents seed variation from being hidden. Standard instance metrics remain complementary. The locked external test shows that an internally stable model may fail before producing any review item. Together, these components form an auditable quality-assurance protocol for comparing agricultural vision queues without assigning them unmeasured deployment value.
